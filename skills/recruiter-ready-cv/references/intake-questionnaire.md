# Comprehensive Resume Intake

Use this checklist as an adaptive interview, not as a form to dump on the candidate. Ask 3--5 related questions per turn, skip verified information, and mark each category Verified, Unknown/skipped, Not applicable, or Conflicting.

## 1. Workflow and source material

- Is this a new resume, an update, or an audit that may become an update?
- What resume, LinkedIn text, portfolio, job description, performance review, project list, or other source material is available?
- Which source is authoritative when dates, titles, or claims disagree?
- What output location and filename does the candidate want?

## 2. Target and application context

- Exact target title, acceptable adjacent titles, seniority, function, and industry.
- Target country or region, preferred locations, remote/hybrid/on-site preference, and relocation constraints.
- Specific vacancy, employer, job description, or representative roles.
- Problems the employer appears to need solved and the candidate's strongest matching evidence.
- Employment type, travel constraints, work authorization, sponsorship needs, clearance, or licensing only when relevant to the target.
- Preferred resume length and market-specific conventions.

## 3. Identity and contact block

- Preferred professional name and optional credentials or pronouns when the candidate wants them shown.
- City, region, and country; do not request a full street address.
- User-approved phone number and professional email.
- LinkedIn, portfolio, GitHub, publication profile, or other relevant links.
- Professional headline aligned with the target.
- Contact details or links that must remain private or be omitted.

## 4. Professional positioning

- How does the candidate describe their profession, domain, and level?
- Which 3--5 capabilities should a recruiter notice first?
- What differentiates the candidate from similarly qualified applicants?
- Which industries, customers, products, populations, or business functions do they understand?
- What career transition, employment gap, overlapping role, or varied experience needs a clear connection?
- Which claims best demonstrate the candidate's value?

## 5. Employment history

Process one role at a time, most recent first.

### Role identity

- Employer, business unit, location, employment type, and whether the employer may be named.
- Official title, externally understandable title if different, promotions, and concurrent roles.
- Start and end month/year, whether current, and explanation needed for gaps or overlaps.
- Reporting line, team position, direct reports, matrix leadership, and stakeholder groups.
- Organization, customer, product, portfolio, geography, budget, revenue, workload, user base, or operational scale.

### Work and contribution

- Mandate: why the role existed and what the candidate owned.
- Recurring responsibilities that are relevant to the target.
- Problems, risks, bottlenecks, or opportunities addressed.
- Actions personally performed versus team contributions.
- Tools, platforms, methods, standards, languages, or domain knowledge used.
- Cross-functional, client, executive, vendor, regulatory, or external collaboration.
- Decisions made, ambiguity handled, constraints overcome, and leadership demonstrated.

### Achievement drill-down

For each important accomplishment ask:

- What was the starting situation or baseline?
- What specific action did the candidate take?
- How large or complex was the work?
- What changed in revenue, cost, time, quality, risk, accuracy, availability, adoption, satisfaction, compliance, throughput, or capacity?
- What metric, before/after value, percentage, count, duration, ranking, award, or stakeholder validation supports the result?
- Was the result measured, estimated, or qualitative, and can it be stated publicly?
- What obstacles or tradeoffs make the achievement meaningful?
- Which target-job requirement does it demonstrate?

If a metric is unavailable, capture verified scope, ownership, complexity, frequency, and qualitative effect. Never create a proxy number.

### Role closeout

- Most important achievements to retain.
- Content that is confidential, outdated, irrelevant, or should be de-emphasized.
- Reason for leaving only if it is needed to understand the chronology; do not place it on the resume by default.

## 6. Projects and portfolio

For each relevant professional, academic, volunteer, freelance, or open-source project:

- Name, dates, context, and link.
- Problem, users, candidate's role, collaborators, and individual contribution.
- Architecture, tools, methods, and difficult decisions.
- Scale, evaluation method, adoption, outcome, or lessons supported by evidence.
- Current status and whether the work can be demonstrated publicly.
- Target-job capability the project proves.

## 7. Skills and tools

- Technical, functional, domain, leadership, communication, and language skills.
- Proficiency or recency only when the candidate can support it.
- Evidence from roles or projects for priority skills.
- Tools the candidate used directly versus only encountered.
- Skills required by the vacancy that are missing, adjacent, or transferable.
- Obsolete, generic, duplicated, or unsupported skills to remove.

## 8. Education and professional development

- Degree or qualification, field, institution, location, and graduation or attendance dates.
- Honors, distinctions, thesis, coursework, activities, or GPA only when relevant and approved.
- Certifications, licenses, issuing organizations, issue/expiry dates, credential IDs or links when appropriate.
- Courses, bootcamps, apprenticeships, or continuing education that strengthen the target.
- In-progress qualifications and accurate expected completion dates.

## 9. Additional evidence

Ask whether each category applies:

- Awards, honors, competitive rankings, grants, scholarships, or internal recognition.
- Publications, patents, talks, conferences, media, teaching, mentoring, or training delivered.
- Professional associations, committees, board service, or community leadership.
- Volunteering and pro bono work with contribution and impact.
- Relevant languages with honest proficiency.
- Military service, security clearance, or professional licensing when relevant and safe to disclose.
- Interests only when strategically useful or specifically requested.

## 10. LinkedIn and supporting consistency

- Does supplied LinkedIn content use the same dates, titles, seniority, and target direction?
- Which profile details add credibility or create contradictions?
- Are portfolio links current and accessible?
- Which resume claims need supporting profile, project, or publication evidence?

Do not claim to have checked a live profile unless the candidate supplied it or authorized access.

## 11. Presentation preferences

- Resume versus CV terminology and target-market expectations.
- Sections to include or omit and preferred ordering.
- A4 versus letter paper.
- Spelling convention and language.
- Required filename and output directory.
- Any accessibility, confidentiality, employer-naming, or redaction constraints.

Use the bundled ATS template by default. Do not add a photo, color, icons, graphics, columns, tables, ratings, progress bars, references, salary information, or protected personal characteristics.

## 12. Completion confirmation

Before drafting, summarize:

- target and positioning;
- contact block approved for publication;
- chronological roles, dates, and titles;
- strongest verified achievements and metrics;
- skills, education, projects, certifications, and applicable additional evidence;
- conflicts resolved;
- unknown/skipped evidence gaps; and
- output and formatting choices.

Ask the candidate to correct or approve this summary. Only then create the final structured resume, LaTeX source, and PDF.
