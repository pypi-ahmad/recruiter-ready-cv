# Audit Job Search Visibility

## Goal

Determine whether the candidate's relevant value is visible before the interview stage. Separate fixable visibility and targeting problems from genuine fit gaps, later-stage interview problems, and conclusions the supplied evidence cannot support.

## Keep Diagnosis Separate from Rewriting

Return the audit and action plan before changing the CV or LinkedIn content. This skill diagnoses only; do not draft or rewrite documents inside this workflow, even when the initial request also asks for a rewrite.

Route unclear professional direction to `$audit-career-positioning`. Route approved CV creation or revision to `$recruiter-ready-cv`. Treat explicit rewrite approval as authorization for that handoff, not as permission to duplicate another skill's work. If the required skill is unavailable, provide a copy-ready handoff prompt instead of drafting the document here.

## Gather the Minimum Evidence

Ask only for missing information:

- primary target role and acceptable adjacent roles;
- application count, time period, interview outcomes, and main application channels;
- three to five representative vacancies or a summary of recurring requirements;
- the CV version used and examples of vacancy-specific changes;
- supplied LinkedIn headline, About section, and recent experience; and
- relevant location, work-authorization, seniority, industry, or required-system constraints.

Do not treat total application volume as proof that the applications were comparable or suitable. Do not claim to have reviewed a live profile unless its content was supplied or access was authorized.

## Run the Audit

### 1. Establish the Funnel Pattern

Identify the repeated failure stage before auditing documents:

- Few or no interviews across genuinely suitable applications supports a pre-interview visibility or targeting investigation.
- Regular interviews without offers indicates that CV visibility is unlikely to be the primary bottleneck.
- Mixed outcomes, a small sample, or missing vacancy evidence is inconclusive.

State the time period, sample limitations, and unknown employer factors. Do not generalize from one rejection.

### 2. Test Application Suitability

Compare the candidate's verified evidence with must-have requirements, expected ownership, seniority, industry context, systems, location, and work authorization. Classify the reviewed sample as:

- **Suitable:** the candidate supplies credible evidence for the core requirements;
- **Partly suitable:** several core requirements are weak, missing, or unclear;
- **Mismatched:** material requirements or constraints are unsupported; or
- **Unknown:** the vacancies or candidate evidence are insufficient.

Do not infer suitability from job titles alone. Separate a communication problem from a real experience or eligibility gap.

### 3. Run the First-Scan Visibility Test

Inspect the headline, summary, top skills, recent role, and earliest prominent achievements before reading deeply. Determine whether a recruiter can quickly identify the target, level, relevant experience, and likely value.

Treat document length as contextual, not as a universal rule. Flag length only when low-value detail hides relevant evidence or makes scanning difficult.

### 4. Audit Evidence and Achievement Visibility

Distinguish duties from demonstrated contribution. Prefer verified evidence containing action, ownership, scope or context, and result. Accept accurate qualitative effects, complexity, stakeholders, scale, or frequency when metrics do not exist.

Identify strong evidence buried below generic summaries, long duty lists, or unrelated experience. Never invent numbers, achievements, employers, tools, or outcomes.

### 5. Audit Vacancy Alignment and Keywords

Extract the recurring priority capabilities and terminology from the representative vacancies. Check whether the CV uses accurate, natural language supported by the candidate's evidence.

Flag generic reuse when the same emphasis is applied to materially different roles. Do not recommend keyword stuffing, hidden text, or unsupported terminology, and do not claim an ATS rejection without evidence.

### 6. Compare CV and LinkedIn Visibility

Check whether supplied CV and LinkedIn content support the same target, level, dates, titles, capabilities, and recent trajectory. Flag incomplete or generic profile content only when it weakens confidence or hides relevant evidence.

Do not require identical wording. Do not expose unnecessary contact details in the audit.

### 7. Evaluate Application Channels and Referrals

Treat referrals as a visibility channel, not as a substitute for credible positioning and evidence. Note channel concentration or low-quality application sources when supported, but do not blame recruiters, ATS systems, referrals, or the job market without evidence.

## Produce the Audit

Return:

1. **Funnel verdict:** State the likely failure stage, confidence, decisive evidence, and material limits.
2. **Application suitability:** Summarize the reviewed sample as Suitable, Partly suitable, Mismatched, or Unknown.
3. **Visibility verdict:** Classify value as Visible, Partly visible, Hidden, or Inconclusive.
4. **Visibility scorecard:** Rate target clarity, relevant experience, achievement evidence, vacancy terminology, presentation, and CV/LinkedIn alignment as Strong, Needs attention, Unsupported, or Unknown. Do not calculate a universal score.
5. **Priority findings:** Rank the few issues most likely to reduce callbacks and cite supplied wording or evidence.
6. **Next-application experiment:** Define one target role family, the evidence and terminology to surface, the documents to align, and the outcomes to track across the next bounded application set.

Do not recommend simply increasing application volume. Do not promise that changes will produce interviews.

## Prepare the Handoff

After the candidate approves further work:

- send target ambiguity and competing role-family findings to `$audit-career-positioning`;
- send verified facts, evidence gaps, visibility priorities, and vacancy alignment needs to `$recruiter-ready-cv`; and
- preserve unknowns and conflicts instead of turning them into claims.

## Guardrails

- Do not make market-specific claims unless the user supplies evidence and requests that analysis.
- Do not assign a universal ATS score or claim knowledge of an employer's screening decision.
- Do not recommend paid services, new qualifications, or an industry change without evidence that they address the diagnosed bottleneck.
- Do not represent correlation between document changes and later outcomes as guaranteed causation.
